Welcome — version 1, polished, and six motions for the gold light on the button

The still first, at full size. Below it the same phone six times, each with one candidate motion on Continue with phone, looping. Open this file in a browser to judge them moving; the toolbar slows everything to a quarter speed.

Every motion is transform + opacity on one layer — nothing animates a shadow, a blur or a layout.

The polish, against version 1

  • A light source. A warm gold haze in the top-right corner the arcs come from, so the arcs read as lit rather than drawn.
  • The discs glow. The front disc carries a faint gold outer glow on its lit side, the back disc a faint mint one — the stack sits in its own light.
  • A shade at the foot. The lowest third of the ground darkens a touch, so the gold hairlines of both buttons sit on something and the primary's fill separates from the ground.
  • The primary button is lit from below — a soft gold under-glow with its black shadow — and keeps its specular top edge.
  • Everything else is version 1 as you saw it: the ground, the arcs, the three badges, the eyebrow, the headline, the two actions, the app's copy.

What makes any of them smooth

  • One moving layer, transform + opacity only. The light is a small View with a gradient fill; it moves by translate and rotate. Its rim stays a static border. Nothing animates a shadow, a blur, a radius or a layout — those re-rasterise every frame.
  • One shared value. A 0 → 1 progress from withRepeat(withTiming(1, {duration: lap, easing: Easing.linear}), -1); the point on the rounded-rectangle path is computed inside useAnimatedStyle — the same parametrisation this page uses, on the UI thread.
  • The rim-only effects use a cover, not a mask. The moving light sits under an inner View painted with the button's own fill, inset 1 pt; only the hairline shows what passes beneath. No MaskedView, no dependency.
  • The pattern already in the repo: a .shared.tsx with the numbers, a .motion120.tsx Reanimated sibling, the base file resolving through resolveMotion120 with the native-driver fallback and the reduced-motion branch — exactly as SuccessMark and the mode transition are built.
  • Reduced motion: the light does not run; the rim rests at its static gold.
  • pointerEvents none on every light layer; the button's touch target is untouched.

My pick

f, the rim glint — it is literally the ask, a gold light slowly circling the button, and because it lives in the hairline the label stays clean and it never reads as a loading indicator. b is the same idea made visible from across the room; a is the quietest. c is the most "premium card" but lights two sides at once, so it is a sweep rather than an orbit. d and e are not orbits at all — they are here as the calmer alternatives, and either pairs well under f.
