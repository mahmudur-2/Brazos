The mode switch — six ideas, live

Each phone loops Customer → Worker and back (the cut back is the loop, not part of the idea). Every idea is transform + opacity on one layer, so it runs on the UI thread at 120 Hz. The real tree swap happens under full cover in every variant — the moment named in each caption.

The confirm step is shown separately below the grid — it is the same in every variant.

The confirm, before any of these

It stays — mode changes only through switchMode() with a confirmation (CLAUDE.md), so the question is how light it can be, not whether. Three shapes, cheapest first:

  1. The Lux dialog you already saw in batch 6 (lux-amount-2): "Switch to Worker Mode?" · Switch / Stay in Customer. Two taps, no motion cost, the copy unchanged.
  2. Inline confirm on the row: the "Switch to Worker" row in the [+] sheet (and the worker's Profile row) expands in place to "Switch to Worker? · Switch" with the mint chip; a second tap on the chip is the confirmation, anywhere else collapses it. One less modal, same guarantee — but it changes the confirm plumbing (`confirmAsync` → a row-owned confirm), so it is a plan of its own.
  3. Hold to switch: press-and-hold the row 600 ms with a mint ring filling around the glyph; release early cancels. Fastest feel, least discoverable; needs a hint ("Hold to switch").

What makes any of them smooth (the part that matters more than the choice)

  • One moving layer, transform + opacity only — no animated blur, shadow, radius or layout. The current sheet already obeys this; what it does not do is next.
  • Reveal when painted, not on a timer. Today the swap fires at 500 ms and the sheet leaves at 700 ms whatever the incoming tree has painted. Instead: swap under full cover, then wait for the incoming tree's first onLayout plus one frame, then reveal — with a 400 ms failsafe. No blank frame can ever show, and on a warm device the reveal comes ~120 ms sooner.
  • Reanimated 4 shared values (installed; the overlay's docblock predates it) — the whole choreography as worklets on the UI thread, the swap a single runOnJS at the cover point.
  • Rasterize the moving pane (shouldRasterizeIOS / renderToHardwareTextureAndroid) for the duration, so a glass pane with a rim and a specular edge composites as one texture.
  • No 1.7 MB artwork — the two transition PNGs go; the mark is the app icon asset at 96 pt, or nothing (b, d and f carry only a word).
  • Reduced motion: a 240 ms crossfade through the ground, in every variant.
  • Direction means something: → to Worker, ← back to Customer (b, d), or grow-from-the-control (c).

My pick

b, the glass wipe — the most Lux (it is the system's own glass, moving), the fastest that still covers the swap (520 ms, never at rest), directional, one layer. f is the strongest cheap add-on to any of them: the bar swap is the one thing that visibly changes between modes, so letting it tell the story reads honest. d is the most "native" but needs both slides to be gated on the paint signal. e is the most branded and the slowest; a is the floor. c is delightful from the [+] but the worker side has no [+], so its return trip grows from the Profile tab instead — asymmetric.

  2. navigation/ModeShell.tsx: the incoming tree's root reports its first layout to the overlay (one callback), nothing else.
  3. modes/ModeProvider.tsx: unchanged for 1; for the inline confirm, the confirm becomes an option the row supplies.
  4. Retire assets/brand/transition-*.png and components/modeTransitionAssets.ts; the fences that pin the choreography's numbers move as constants with their dated reason.
  5. Prove it: npm run verify, then the phone — a frame-time trace of ten switches on the Beta (no dropped frames at 120 Hz), and the two-commit swap still never shows a blank frame.
